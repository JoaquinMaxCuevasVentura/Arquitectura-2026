## 1. Price per still render (exterior / interior / aerial / night) by LatAm market and on global freelance platforms

### Takeaway
In neighboring LatAm markets, published studio prices for one professional still cluster around **USD 60–400 per image**. Peru lists S/200–700 (≈USD 60–210). Argentina lists USD 90–100 per image at a mid-size studio, and a 2026 guide gives USD 100–400. Chile lists CLP 150k–500k (≈USD 160–540). Colombia lists COP 1.0–1.15M (≈USD 340–365) for the first image *including modeling*, with extra views from ≈USD 66. Mexico's Habitissimo marketplace shows MXN 1,300–1,800 (≈USD 70–100) per image. Ecuador lists USD 60–1,200. Global freelance platforms span from **USD 10–55 entry Fiverr gigs** (Lumion/D5) to **USD 200–1,000 per project** on Upwork. US/EU-facing studios charge **USD 199–5,000+ per image**, and aerials and large commercial exteriors sit at the top of that range.

### Inferences
- A defensible "neighboring-market" band for one professional real-time or mid-tier still of a public building, when the model is supplied (Revit), is roughly **USD 90–350 per image**. The low end comes from Argentina/Mexico/Ecuador (USD 60–100), the middle from Peru/Colombia extra views (USD 66–210), and the upper end from Chile exterior/aerial (up to USD 540) and Colombia first-image-with-modeling (≈USD 340–365).
- Across sources, aerial views carry a premium of about 35–50%. Night views are priced the same as day exteriors in at least one LatAm list (Habitissimo MX: MXN 1,800 for both), but as a premium in US guides.
- Prices that include 3D modeling (Colombia's first image) are 3–5× higher than "additional view" prices (COP 1.08M vs COP 208k). Because the La Paz client already has a Revit model, the relevant comparator is closer to the "additional view" or "model supplied" price, plus scene setup and entourage.
- Fiverr's $10–55 D5/Lumion starting prices are a floor for single, simple views. They do not represent the cost of a large public building with many views.

## 2. Walkthrough / fly-through animation pricing: per second, per minute, per package; scaling with length, resolution, fps, entourage, post-production, music, voice-over

### Takeaway
In LatAm, published 1-minute Full HD walkthroughs cost about **USD 1,000–1,700** (Colombia COP 3.8–5.4M; Chile from CLP 1M for 30–60 s; Mexico about MXN 6,000 per 20 s ≈ USD 1,000/min). Some studios instead price video as **+60% of the render budget** (Argentina). Colombian lists show strong economies of scale: 2 minutes for ≈1.56× the price of 1 minute. Global guides put studio animation at **USD 2,000–15,000+ per finished minute (~USD 50–200/s)**, with "basic flythroughs" at USD 2,200–5,000/min. Upwork lists walkthrough projects at USD 3,000–10,000, and Fiverr Lumion/D5 walkthrough gigs start at USD 10–100. Resolution and frame rate drive cost mainly through render compute. I found no published per-item prices for voice-over, music, or entourage.

### Inferences
- **Scaling with length is sub-linear in LatAm studio lists.** Arca Digital charges ≈1.56× for 2 min vs 1 min, so the second minute costs about 56% of the first. This is consistent with fixed setup costs (scene, materials, entourage, camera planning) being front-loaded. Extrapolating that pattern to 3–5 minutes is my inference, not a published figure; a 3-minute piece might land around 2.0–2.2× the 1-minute price.
- Short clips cost more per second than long ones. Maverick Frame's 15–30 s teasers at $800–1,200 work out to $27–80/s, while its basic flythroughs at $2,200–5,000/min work out to $37–83/s.
- Mapping LatAm per-minute studio prices (≈USD 1,000–1,700 per 1080p minute) onto RealSpace3D's tiers places them in the "bottom tier" ($10–100/s, here ≈$17–28/s). That is consistent with real-time-engine production. One Colombian studio explicitly uses Lumion for a 1-minute, 2-day-delivery tour.
- Going from 1080p to 4K adds roughly a third in the one source that splits the two (Chasing Illusions). Going from 30 to 60 fps doubles frames, and therefore render compute, at the same resolution. With real-time engines (D5/Lumion) the compute penalty is far smaller than with offline V-Ray/Corona, so the price premium is mostly about client expectations.
- Voice-over, licensed music, and heavy animated entourage (moving buses, crowds) are not priced as separate line items in any source found. They are bundled into "marketing-grade" or "post-production" tiers ($8,000–18,000/min at Maverick Frame).

### Gaps
- I found no published separate prices for: voice-over (locución), licensed music, animated entourage (people/buses/vehicles), motion graphics/titles, or color grading for arch-viz walkthroughs.
- No source gave explicit per-minute prices at 3 or 5 minutes. Scaling beyond 2 minutes is inferred.
- I found no LatAm source giving explicit 1080p vs 4K price differentials for video.

## 3. Real-time engines (D5/Lumion/Enscape/Twinmotion) vs offline photoreal (V-Ray/Corona), and how AI-enhanced renders are priced (scope update)

### Takeaway
No source publishes an explicit two-tier price list (real-time vs offline). The evidence is indirect but consistent. D5/Lumion/Twinmotion work dominates the cheapest offers (Fiverr gigs from $10–55; LatAm 1-minute Lumion tours delivered in 2 days). Premium studio guides ($250–5,000/image) are associated with V-Ray/Corona-grade photorealism. On AI, I found **no evidence of studios charging a separate line item for AI enhancement or upscaling**. One Fiverr gig sells AI enhancement of an existing render as a standalone service ($125). AI-tool vendors claim prices have "collapsed" ($0–5 per AI image vs $40–400 for outsourced traditional renders). Independent surveys show AI is mainly used for concepting and image enhancement, with quality reliability as the main barrier. No hard data shows AI has pushed down market prices for professional arch-viz deliverables.

### Cited Findings

**Real-time vs offline tiers**
- Lumion is described as "not physically accurate with recognizably 'Lumion' style results… not suitable for photorealistic production rendering, though it leads on exteriors and animation". V-Ray leads on physically precise final-quality interiors; Corona is known for photorealism out of the box — [MyArchitectAI, V-Ray vs Lumion 2026](https://www.myarchitectai.com/blog/lumion-vs-vray); [Maverick Frame, Corona vs V-Ray](https://maverickframe.com/blog/corona-vs-v-ray/) [attribution unverified among these; this is opinion]

- Lumion renders images in seconds and animations in minutes (it needs a heavy GPU), whereas V-Ray's high-quality renders take much longer to compute — [MyArchitectAI](https://www.myarchitectai.com/blog/lumion-vs-vray)

**AI's effect on the market: independent or less conflicted evidence**
- Chaos + Architizer 2026 survey (~800 architects/designers):
  - 64% have experimented with AI tools
  - Among AI users, 48% cite concept design as the biggest time saver and **40% point to image enhancement**
  - 48% cite poor or unreliable output quality as the single biggest obstacle
  - Source: [Dezeen, May 2026](https://www.dezeen.com/2026/05/14/chaos-survey-artificial-intelligence-architecture/); [Chaos blog, AI in architecture research](https://blog.chaos.com/ai-in-architecture-research)
- Ravelin3D (an arch-viz studio): AI "has genuinely transformed specific aspects of the rendering workflow, but not in dramatic, job-replacing ways". ### Inferences
- In practice the market treats AI enhancement (Krea/Magnific/SD) as an **internal production step**. Its cost to the studio is tens of dollars a month. Studios bundle it into the per-image price rather than invoicing it separately. The only standalone pricing found is a Fiverr gig ($125), and it targets clients who already have a render.
- For a La Paz quote built on D5 + Photoshop + AI enhancement, the pipeline maps onto the **real-time/mid tier**. The fair external comparators are LatAm studio prices (USD 90–350/image) and Upwork's $200–1,000 project bands, not US offline-photoreal studio prices ($500–5,000).
- AI enhancement could justify pricing at the upper end of the real-time tier (closer to offline-photoreal look), but I found no market evidence of a formal "AI premium". The risk factor to flag in contract terms is AI altering geometry or materials, which matters for a public tender where fidelity to the Revit model is required.
- There is no hard price-index evidence that AI has lowered professional arch-viz prices in 2025–2026. The "price collapse" claims come from AI-tool vendors and refer to self-serve AI images, not commissioned deliverables.

### Gaps
- I found no studio or freelancer rate card (LatAm or global) that lists "AI enhancement" or "AI upscaling" as a separate fee on top of a render.
- I found no published two-tier price list (real-time vs V-Ray/Corona) from the same studio.
- I found no Workana listings specific to D5 (Workana pages could not be fetched).
- I found no longitudinal price data (e.g., 2023 vs 2026) to test whether AI has depressed prices. The only historical survey is CGarchitect 2009 (see Q7).

## 4. Pricing structures and contract terms: revisions, extra revision fees, deposit (anticipo), turnaround, rush fees, licensing/usage rights, raw-file delivery

### Takeaway
Common terms across LatAm and international sources:
- **Deposit:** 50% up front and 50% on delivery.
- **Revisions:** 1–3 rounds included, typically 2. Minor changes (colors, materials) are free; structural changes are charged.
- **Extra revisions:** about USD 75–400 per round internationally.
- **Rush:** +25–50% for under 48 hours to under 5 business days.
- **Turnaround:** 3–6 days for stills; 1–4 weeks for a 1-minute animation (20 days for 1 min and 28 days for 2 min at one Colombian studio).
- **Usage rights:** commercial or marketing use can cost more than internal use.
- **Source files:** often charged extra or not released.

### Cited Findings
- **Anticipo:** a 50% advance is commonly requested before starting, covering initial modeling and rendering costs. Some studios work 50% advance / 50% on satisfactory delivery — LatAm render-pricing pages including [Legacy State](https://legacystate.com.mx/renders/arquitectura/), [DP Renders 2024](https://www.dprenders.com/l/tarifas-actualizadas-tendencias/), [Render Bogotá precios](https://www.renderbogota.com/precios.html), [Matearq, cómo cobrar un render](https://matearq.com/renders/como-cobrar-por-un-render-de-arquitectura-guia-simple/) [attribution unverified among these]
- **Revisions (LatAm):** most studios include 2–3 rounds of corrections. Minor changes such as colors and materials are typically free; structural changes (moving walls, changing furniture) may incur extra cost. Services typically include modeling, lighting, materials, rendering, post-production and two revision rounds, with additional changes quoted separately — same set of sources as above [attribution unverified]
- Ecuadorian packs state their included changes explicitly: "1 cambio por imagen" (USD 430 pack) and "2 cambios por imagen" (USD 880 pack) — [Anima Audiovisual](https://www.animaudiovisual.com/) [attribution unverified]
- **Revisions (international):** standard projects include one or two rounds free. Third and later rounds cost about $75–200 each; another source says extra revisions cost $100–400 each — [Maverick Frame](https://maverickframe.com/blog/3d-rendering-pricing/); [Renderahouse](https://www.renderahouse.com/architectural-rendering-services); [3D Praxis](https://3dpraxisstudio.com/journal/architectural-rendering-cost-breakdown/) [attribution unverified among these]
- **Rush fees:** rush delivery adds 30–50% to cover overtime and external render-farm costs. RealSpace3D puts a 48-hour turnaround at a 25–50% premium, and Rapid Renders applies 30–50% to anything under five business days — [RealSpace3D rendering pricing guide](https://www.realspace3d.com/resources/3d-rendering-pricing-guide/); [Rapid Renders 2026](https://rapidrenders.com/3d-rendering-pricing/)

Some studios price differently for internal review versus public campaigns" — [Eos Visions 2026](https://eosvisions.com/architectural-rendering-pricing-2026/); [ArchiVinci](https://www.archivinci.com/blogs/how-architects-determine-rendering-costs) [attribution unverified among these]
- **Source / raw files:** "Want the actual 3D model files to reuse later? Many studios charge extra — or won't release them at all" — same international guide set [attribution unverified]
- **Turnaround examples:**
  - Render3DQuick: interiors, exteriors and floor plans in 3–4 days; aerials in 5–6 days; animation in 1–2 weeks — [Render3DQuick](https://render3dquick.com/blog/cuanto-cuesta-el-renderizado-3d)
  - Arca Digital: 1-minute video in 20 days, 2-minute video in 28 days — [Arca Digital](https://arquitectura.arcadigital.co/precios/)
  - Arquimendez: 1-minute Lumion tour in 2 days — [Arquimendez](https://www.arquimendez.com/precios-y-tarifas-de-renders-bogota)
  - Chasing Illusions (US-market): 4–6 weeks — [Chasing Illusions](https://www.chasingillusions.com/blog/how-much-should-video-architectural-walkthroughs-cost-in-2025)
  - Cad Crowd: 2–4 weeks for "standard" animation — [Cad Crowd](https://www.cadcrowd.com/blog/what-are-architectural-animation-costs-rates-3d-design-pricing-for-companies/)

  - DP Renders' 5-render promo saves about 20% — [DP Renders](https://www.dprenders.com/l/precios-renders-argentina-2025-dprenders/)
  - Legacy State offers up to 20% off single renders and 50% off render + modeling packages — [Legacy State](https://legacystate.com.mx/renders/arquitectura/)
  - Bundling day/night variants or extra angles reduces per-image cost by 10–25% because the base model is reused — [Cylind 2026](https://www.cylind.com/articles/how-much-does-a-3d-rendering-cost) [attribution unverified]

### Inferences
- A standard LatAm-compatible term sheet would be: 50/50 payment; 2 included revision rounds per image or video, with minor and major changes defined; per-round fees for extras; a +30–50% rush surcharge; usage limited to the tender/presentation unless broader rights are agreed; and source/project files priced separately or excluded.

### Gaps
- I found no LatAm source with a numeric fee per extra revision round.
- I found no LatAm source pricing usage-rights licensing or raw-file delivery.
- The specific rush-fee percentages found are international (US-facing); none were LatAm-specific.

## 5. Large / public / commercial buildings (terminals, airports, malls) vs residential: complexity multipliers and per-m² approaches

### Takeaway
International guides price large commercial or public projects at **roughly 1.5–3× residential per image**: large-project exteriors $1,200–5,000 vs small $400–1,500, and commercial exteriors $1,500–4,500 vs single-building $500–1,500. Whole large-scale or urban packages run **$20,000–50,000+**. LatAm lists show the same pattern through modeling fees: in Colombia, modeling a building costs 2× less than modeling a residential complex (COP 3M vs 6M), and extra views of larger projects cost more (COP 576k vs 768k). Per-m² pricing exists in Mexico (Legacy State prices "by the project's m²") but no rate per m² was published. I found no terminal- or airport-specific price.

## 6. Reasonable number of images and video length for a public-infrastructure presentation or tender

### Takeaway
No source gives a normative number. Market packages and real requests suggest a typical presentation set of **5–10 stills** (mixing exterior day, aerial, night/dusk and key interiors), sometimes plus 360° panoramas, and a **30-second to 3-minute walkthrough**, with 1–2 minutes the most commonly packaged length. Competition panels require high-resolution stills (A1/A0 at 300 dpi), and video is optional but valued by evaluators.

### Cited Findings
- A real Mexican request quoted on Habitissimo asked for 2 exterior renders, 4 interior renders, 1 night exterior render, one 360° view, and **a virtual tour of about 3 minutes** — [Habitissimo renders 3D](https://www.habitissimo.com.mx/presupuesto/renders-3d)
- Package compositions in LatAm studios:
  - Estudio LAT "Pack Building" for advanced projects: 10 renders + 90-second interior/exterior animation + 5 360° images (US$1,665) — [Estudio LAT](https://www.estudiolatarq.com/precios/)
  - Arquimendez Combo 1: 1-minute Full HD video + 5 interiors + 1 exterior 4K. Combo 3: 8 interiors + 1 exterior + 3 floor plans — [Arquimendez](https://www.arquimendez.com/precios-y-tarifas-de-renders-bogota)
  - Ecuadorian real-estate pack: 7 renders + 2 360° images + 30-second video — [Anima Audiovisual](https://www.animaudiovisual.com/) [attribution unverified]
  - Arca Digital packages video at 1 minute or 2 minutes — [Arca Digital](https://arquitectura.arcadigital.co/precios/)
- Competitions and tenders: high-resolution renders are generally required for panels (300 dpi, A1 or A0), along with versions for digital presentation and in some cases animations. Tenders may include a technical report and descriptive panels with renders, plus "una propuesta gráfica clara incluyendo video, que facilita la comprensión del diseño por parte de los técnicos evaluadores" — [Viseni, concursos y licitaciones](https://www.viseni.com/visualizacion/concursos-publicos-arquitectura); [Luafix, renders para concurso](https://luafix.es/servicios/renders-para-concurso/); [Domingo Loro, obra pública](https://www.domingoloro.com/portfolio-renders-3d/1344-renders-3d-para-concursos-de-arquitectura-y-obra-publica) [attribution unverified among these]
- Viseni's portfolio includes public competitions awarded by ADIF (Spain's rail infrastructure agency). It "define las vistas más estratégicas" based on the competition's evaluation criteria — [Viseni](https://www.viseni.com/visualizacion/concursos-publicos-arquitectura)
- Common marketing video lengths: social teasers 15–30 s and website hero loops 30–60 s — [Maverick Frame](https://maverickframe.com/blog/3d-walkthrough-animation-cost/)
- Day and dusk views, alternative perspectives, and a mix of exterior and interior views are commonly needed to communicate scale, context and spatial logic — [Interface Multimedia](https://ifmm.com/insights/architectural-rendering-cost-guide/) [attribution unverified]

### Inferences
- For a bus terminal, a typical tender or presentation set consistent with these packages would be about 6–10 stills: an aerial context view, a plaza/main façade view by day, the same by night or dusk, the platforms/andenes with buses, the waiting hall, the ticket counters, and possibly a commercial or food area. It would be accompanied by a 1–3 minute walkthrough. This is my synthesis; no source prescribes it for terminals.
- A video longer than 2–3 minutes goes beyond what LatAm studios package as standard. Cost should be expected to rise sub-linearly, and evaluators' attention span argues against over-long videos (inference).

### Gaps
- I found no Latin American public tender (pliego/bases) for a terminal that specifies the number of renders or the video length.
- I found no survey on the optimal video length for public-infrastructure presentations.

### Inferences
- Treat every 2025–2026 "price guide" as seller-side marketing. They tend to anchor high (US-market studio prices), and several are written by AI-tool vendors that anchor "traditional" prices high to sell subscriptions. Their wide ranges ($250–10,000 per image; $1,000–90,000 per minute) are only useful as outer bounds.
- The most useful benchmarks for La Paz are the LatAm published rate cards: Estudio LAT (USD), Arca Digital and Arquimendez (COP), Crossmind (CLP), Habitissimo MX (MXN), and the Ecuador USD packs. Upwork's platform-wide project bands are the most neutral global freelance reference.
